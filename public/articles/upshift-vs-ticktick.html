Habits and quests vs tasks and planning

TickTick sits in the task manager category, with extras around it. It is a good fit when you want lists, dates and a calendar in one place, and it also includes habit tracking. Upshift starts from the other side: habits first, with a task-like feature added.

In Upshift, quests are your main daily habits you want to build long term. Sidequests are daily challenges generated by the app for extra XP. One-day quests work like a to-do list and can be scheduled for future days, weekly and monthly quests run on the days you pick, quit quests track habits you want to break, and roulette quests add random challenges. Explore helps you find new habits. Smart reminders nudge you to complete them.

Upshift also supports longer plans. You set a long-term goal, break it into micro-goals and keep a journal with your plan and reflections. The time tracker records your focus sessions and daily activity on a calendar, keeps your day streak alive and lets you look back at your history.

App blocking and screen time

TickTick helps you plan your time, but planning does not control your phone. It is not an app blocker.

Upshift connects your habits to your screen time. The Quest Blocker keeps distracting apps blocked until you finish your daily quests. Block Now blocks apps instantly when you need to focus. Time Limits cap daily usage for apps and websites, and Work Schedule blocks distracting apps during work hours. Pre-built blocks such as the porn blocker are included, each with its own custom overlay. With strict mode on, a block cannot be bypassed until the app is unblocked, depending on the block type.

The blocker is built on Apple's Screen Time technology, so Upshift cannot see your screen time data. For a walkthrough, see how to block apps on iPhone or how to limit screen time on iPhone.

Motivation: XP, levels and streaks

Upshift gives each completed quest XP in Money, Strength, Health or Knowledge. XP raises your global level, unlocks badges and achievements, and shows on your profile with your stats. Streak and day celebrations appear when you stay consistent. The biggest incentive is the unlock: your blocked apps return once your quests are done.

Task managers motivate mostly through order and clarity. Seeing a plan for the day helps, but there is no built-in consequence if you ignore it. If you know what to do and still do not do it, Upshift's blocker addresses that gap directly. More on this in why you feel unmotivated.

Who each app is best for

Upshift is best for

  • People who want a game-like system for building daily habits.
  • People who get pulled into social media or games and want blocks tied to their quests.
  • People who want habits, goals and a time tracker in one place.
  • People who like accountability through friends and leaderboards.

TickTick is best for

  • People who want one tool for tasks, calendar planning and light habit tracking.
  • People who manage many tasks with dates and reminders.
  • People who prefer a planner to a gamified system.

When TickTick is the better choice

TickTick is the better choice if planning is your main need. If your days are full of tasks and deadlines, a planner with calendar views will serve you better than a habit app. It is also a good pick if you want a single tool that covers lists, scheduling and a few habits without any blocking or game features. Upshift's one-day quests are handy, but Upshift is not meant to replace a full planner.

If you want both, you can plan in a task app and use Upshift to build habits and protect your focus.
