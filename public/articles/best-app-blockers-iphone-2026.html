An app blocker stops you from opening distracting apps or sites, either completely, on a schedule, or after a time limit. The best one for you depends on how strict you need to be and whether you want a pure blocker or one that also builds better habits. Below are seven options for iPhone, from the free built-in tool to dedicated focus apps.

What to look for in an app blocker

  • Blocking modes: instant blocks, daily time limits, schedules, and conditions you must meet first.
  • Strictness: can you disable the block in two taps? A blocker you can easily bypass tends to fail when willpower is lowest.
  • Websites and apps: distraction often lives in the browser as well as in apps.
  • Privacy: blockers need access to your usage. Prefer tools built on Apple's Screen Time technology, which is designed with privacy in mind.
  • Habit and progress features: reminders, streaks and tracking help the change last after the blocking.
  • Free access and platforms: pricing and availability change, so check each store page.

The list

1. Apple Screen Time

Screen Time is built into iOS and costs nothing to try. It offers App Limits, Downtime and content restrictions, and it can set a passcode so limits are harder to change. It is the obvious first step, though it is fairly basic and its limits are easy to ignore if you set the passcode yourself.

Best for: people who want a free, built-in option and do not want to install anything. See our guide to limiting screen time on iPhone.

2. Upshift

Upshift is a habit tracker and app blocker in one. Its signature feature is the Quest Blocker: distracting apps stay blocked until you finish your daily quests. Beyond that you can use Block Now to block apps instantly, Time Limits to cap daily usage of apps and websites, and a Work Schedule to block apps during work hours. There are also pre-built blocks, such as adult content and gambling blocks, and each block type has its own custom overlay. With strict mode on, a block cannot be bypassed until the app is unblocked (depending on the block type).

Upshift is built on top of Apple's Screen Time technology, so it cannot view your screen time data. You also get quests, XP, goals with a journal, a time tracker, friends and leaderboards. It is on the App Store and Google Play, and offers a 7-day free trial. On Android, check the store page for what blocking is supported, since it is not identical to iPhone.

Best for: people who want blocking tied to real habits. Our pick for earning your screen time.

3. Opal

Opal is a focus app known for polished design and focus sessions that block distracting apps. It leans into building a focus routine, with scheduling and progress insights. It is a strong choice if your goal is deep work sessions rather than habit tracking.

Best for: people who want a focused, design-forward blocker for work sessions. Check the store page for current pricing and platforms.

4. One Sec

One Sec takes a different approach. Instead of hard blocking, it adds a short pause and a breathing moment before a chosen app opens, which breaks the automatic habit of reaching for it. You can still continue if you decide you really want to.

Best for: people who open apps on autopilot and want friction instead of a wall.

5. ScreenZen

ScreenZen adds a delay and mindful prompts when you open apps you choose, and supports limits for those apps. It aims to create awareness rather than lock you out, and is known for offering a free option. Verify current features and pricing on the store page.

Best for: people who want a lightweight, awareness-based approach and want to start free.

6. Freedom

Freedom is a long-running blocker that works across devices, blocking apps and websites on a schedule or for a session. Because it can sync across devices, it is helpful if you get distracted on your phone and computer.

Best for: people who need the same blocks on phone and computer.

7. Forest

Forest is a focus timer. You plant a virtual tree and it grows while you stay off your phone; leaving the app kills it. It is more of a motivation tool than a hard blocker, but the visual reward works well for many people, especially students.

Best for: students and people who respond to gentle, visual motivation.

How to choose

Decide how much friction you need. If you only slip occasionally, a pause app like One Sec or ScreenZen may be enough. If you keep breaking your own rules, you need a strict block such as Upshift with strict mode, or a focus app like Opal. If you want a free starting point, try Apple Screen Time first and see where it falls short.

Think about what you want after the block. A blocker that tells you nothing about what to do instead leaves a gap. Linking blocks to goals or habits, as Upshift does with quests, fills that gap. You can read more about the evidence in our article on how app blockers work.

FAQ

Does iPhone have a built-in app blocker?

Yes. Apple Screen Time lets you set App Limits and Downtime. Third-party apps add more modes, stricter options and habit features.

Can I get around a block?

It depends on the app and settings. Built-in limits can often be dismissed. With Upshift's strict mode on, a block cannot be bypassed until the app is unblocked (depending on the block type).

Is it safe to give an app blocker access to my screen time?

Apps built on Apple's Screen Time technology work within Apple's privacy design. Upshift is built this way, which means it cannot view your screen time data.

Are there free app blockers?

Apple Screen Time is free and built in. Several third-party blockers offer a free tier or trial, but this changes, so verify on the store page. Upshift offers a 7-day free trial.

Should I block apps or just set time limits?

Time limits suit apps you use for a mix of reasons. Full blocks suit apps that mostly waste time. Many people use both, along with a schedule for work hours.
