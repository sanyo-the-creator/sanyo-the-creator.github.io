The best habit tracker for your iPhone is the one you will still open in three weeks. For some people that is a clean checklist with streaks. For others it is a game, a coach, or an app that also blocks the distractions that break their routine. This list covers seven apps that take different approaches, so you can match the tool to how you actually work.

What to look for in a habit tracker

  • Habits vs tasks: a habit repeats and is judged by consistency. A task is done once. Some apps handle both, others focus on only one.
  • Reminders: a habit you forget is a habit you skip. Look for flexible, well-timed reminders.
  • Progress tracking: streaks, calendars, stats and history show you whether the habit is sticking.
  • Motivation style: clean and minimal, game-like, or coaching-led. Pick the one that matches your personality.
  • Focus and blocking: if your phone is the reason you miss habits, an app that also limits distracting apps can help.
  • Free access and platforms: pricing and free tiers change often. Check each store page for current details and make sure the app runs on all the devices you use.

The list

1. Streaks

Streaks is a focused habit tracker built for Apple devices. It keeps things simple: you pick the habits you want to build and mark them done each day, and the app keeps your streak count front and center. It fits naturally into the Apple ecosystem with widgets and Apple Watch support.

Best for: people who want a minimal, no-fuss tracker and use Apple devices only. Our pick for a clean streak-focused experience.

2. Upshift

Upshift turns your habits into quests. You can create one day quests that work like a to-do list, weekly and monthly quests that run on the days you choose, quit quests to track bad habits you want to break, and roulette quests that throw random challenges at you. Every quest you complete earns XP in Money, Strength, Health or Knowledge, which raises your level and unlocks badges. Smart reminders nudge you to finish your habits, and a daily sidequest adds bonus XP.

What makes it different from a plain tracker is the Quest Blocker: distracting apps stay blocked until your daily quests are done. It is built on Apple Screen Time, and you can add Block Now, Time Limits and a Work Schedule. It also includes goals with a journal, a time tracker, friends, groups and leaderboards, 9 themes and home screen widgets. Upshift is on the App Store and Google Play and offers a 7-day free trial.

Best for: people who get distracted by their phone and want their habits to actually gate their screen time. Our pick for habits plus accountability.

3. Habitify

Habitify is a dedicated habit tracker with a clean interface, flexible schedules, reminders and progress statistics. It is available across several platforms, which helps if you move between phone and computer. It is closer to a classic tracker than a game.

Best for: people who want detailed habit stats and a polished tracker on more than one device.

4. Habitica

Habitica turns your habits, daily tasks and to-dos into a role-playing game. You build an avatar, and completing real-life tasks earns rewards while missing them has in-game consequences. You can also join parties with friends. It is playful and quite different from minimal trackers.

Best for: people who love role-playing games and respond well to rewards and penalties.

5. TickTick

TickTick is a task manager that also includes a habit tracker and a focus timer. That makes it useful if you want your tasks, calendar and habits in one place instead of juggling several apps. The habit side is simpler than in dedicated trackers.

Best for: people who want one app for tasks, planning and a basic habit tracker.

6. Fabulous

Fabulous takes a coaching approach. Instead of asking you to build a list of habits on your own, it guides you through routines and small steps, often around morning, energy and sleep. It feels more like a wellness program than a spreadsheet of checkboxes.

Best for: beginners who want guidance and structure rather than a blank tracker.

7. Finch

Finch is a self-care app built around a virtual pet. You complete small daily tasks and check in on your mood, and your pet grows as you do. The tone is gentle and encouraging, which suits people who feel pressured by strict streaks.

Best for: people who want a low-pressure, self-care-oriented approach to habits.

How to choose

Start with the problem you want to solve. If you only need a daily checklist, a minimal tracker like Streaks or Habitify is enough. If you need extra motivation, a game-style app like Habitica or Upshift gives you something to chase. If you want a guide, try Fabulous. If your struggle is mostly self-care and mood, Finch may fit better.

Then check the practical details: does it run on every device you use, does it remind you at the right time, and is the free access enough for your needs? Pricing and limits change, so read the current store page before you commit. Finally, try one app for two weeks. Switching constantly is a bigger risk than picking the slightly wrong app.

FAQ

What is the difference between a habit tracker and a to-do app?

A to-do app tracks tasks you finish once. A habit tracker tracks things you repeat and shows your consistency over time with streaks and history. Some apps, including Upshift and TickTick, cover both.

Do I need a paid habit tracker?

Not necessarily. Many habit trackers offer a free tier or trial, but terms change. Check each app's store page for current pricing. Upshift offers a 7-day free trial.

How many habits should I track at once?

Start with a few. A short list you complete reliably builds more momentum than a long list you abandon.

Can a habit tracker help with phone distraction?

Trackers help you remember and measure habits. To actually cut distractions, you need an app that limits or blocks other apps. Upshift combines both: its Quest Blocker keeps chosen apps blocked until your daily quests are done.

Which habit tracker works on both iPhone and Android?

Several do, including Upshift, Habitify, Habitica, TickTick and Finch. Streaks is built for Apple devices. Always confirm platform availability on the store page.
