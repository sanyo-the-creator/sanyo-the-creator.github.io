Quest Blocker blocks distracting apps until you complete your daily quests. It is Upshift's signature feature: your important tasks come first, and entertainment apps unlock afterward. This page explains how it works, how to set it up in general steps, and how it differs from plain time limits.

Last updated November 2026.

What Quest Blocker Does

You pick the apps that distract you most and you set your daily quests. Until your quests are done, those apps stay blocked. Once you finish, you earn access. It turns your phone from something that steals your time into something that rewards your progress.

The idea behind Upshift is simple: you open your phone for one minute and lose an hour. With Quest Blocker, the apps stay locked until your quests are done.

First, Understand Quests

Quest Blocker depends on your quests, so it helps to know the basics.

  • Quests are your main daily habits that you want to build long-term.
  • Sidequests are daily challenges generated by the app for extra XP.
  • Quest types include one day quests (like a to-do list), weekly and monthly quests that run on the days you pick, quit quests that track bad habits daily, and roulette quests that give random challenges.

Completing quests earns XP in Money, Strength, Health or Knowledge, raises your level and builds streaks. See What Is Gamified Habit Tracking for the concept.

How to Set Up Quest Blocker

The exact screens may change between app versions, so follow the prompts in the app. In general:

  1. Install Upshift from the App Store or Google Play and complete the onboarding steps.
  2. Create the quests you want to complete every day. Start with a few you can realistically finish.
  3. Open the Focus page and go to the App Blocker.
  4. Choose Quest Blocker.
  5. Select the apps you want blocked until your quests are done.
  6. Optionally turn on strict mode (see below).
  7. Complete your quests and check that the blocked apps unlock.

If you want help choosing which apps to block, see How To Block Apps On iPhone.

Quest Blocker vs Plain Time Limits

A time limit says: you can use this app for a set amount each day. Quest Blocker says: you can use this app after you do the work. They solve different problems.

  • Time Limits cap daily usage for apps and websites. They control how much.
  • Quest Blocker controls when. The apps stay blocked until the daily quests are complete.

A time limit can still let you spend your first free minutes on entertainment and leave important tasks for later. Quest Blocker flips the order. If you want only a cap, see How To Limit Screen Time On iPhone.

Other Blocks You Can Combine

Quest Blocker is one of several block types. You can add more when you need them:

  • Block Now instantly blocks apps when you need to focus.
  • Time Limits cap daily usage for apps and websites.
  • Work Schedule blocks distracting apps during work hours.
  • Pre-built blocks, such as the adult content block and the gambling block.

Each block type has its own custom overlay, which is what you see when you open a blocked app.

Strict Mode

With strict mode on, a block cannot be bypassed until the app is unblocked (depending on the block type). That is the point of it. If you know you are likely to disable a block when you feel tempted, strict mode removes that option. Because it is restrictive, use it when you are sure you want it.

Privacy: Upshift Cannot See Your Screen Time

Upshift is built on top of Apple's Screen Time technology. Apple built it with privacy in mind, so Upshift cannot view your screen time even if it wanted to. You do not have to trust Upshift with that data.

On Android, check the Google Play listing for details. See Upshift on Android.

Tips for Using Quest Blocker Well

  • Keep the quest list realistic. If you cannot finish your quests, you will be locked out and frustrated.
  • Block the apps that cost you the most time. You do not need to block everything.
  • Use Explore to find habits worth turning into quests.
  • Add friends. Accountability helps, and groups have shared challenges and leaderboards.
