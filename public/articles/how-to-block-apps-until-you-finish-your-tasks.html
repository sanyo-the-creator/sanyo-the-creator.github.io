To block apps until you finish your tasks, pick a short list of must-do tasks, choose the apps that distract you most, and set a rule that those apps stay locked until the tasks are checked off. You can do this with a manual routine, but an app that enforces the block for you is much more reliable, because the rule is decided in advance and not renegotiated each time you feel tempted.

Why "tasks first, apps later" works

Most people do the easy, enjoyable thing first and the important thing "later". The idea of earning your entertainment reverses that order. Finishing the task becomes the key that opens the reward, so the pleasant thing supports the productive one instead of competing with it. It also removes the daily decision of whether to start, which is where most procrastination happens.

Step 1: Choose two to four tasks that matter

Keep the list short. If it takes all day to complete, the lock stays on all day and the system feels punishing. Good examples:

  • Finish one focused study or work block.
  • Do a workout or a walk.
  • Make your bed and tidy your desk.
  • Write tomorrow's plan or do ten minutes of reading.

Step 2: Make each task concrete and checkable

"Be productive" cannot be checked off. "Study for 25 minutes" can. A clear finish line makes it easy to know when the lock opens and prevents arguments with yourself.

Step 3: Pick the apps to lock

Choose the apps that eat your time, not every app on your phone. Keep calls, messages from family, maps and anything you need for safety or work unlocked. A common starting list is social media, short-video apps, games and streaming. Start with two or three apps and add more later if needed.

Step 4: Set the rule and the window

Decide when the lock applies. Many people lock distracting apps from morning until their tasks are done. Others use it only during work hours. Be specific: "From wake-up until my three tasks are done."

Step 5: Make the lock hard to cheat

A lock you can switch off in two taps will not survive a bad day. Choose a method that is hard to bypass. Ask a friend to hold a passcode, or use an app with a strict mode, so the block cannot be undone in a weak moment.

Step 6: Keep the tasks small enough to win

If you fail to unlock your apps for several days in a row, shrink the tasks. The system should feel fair. A win every day builds the habit, while an impossible list builds resentment.

Step 7: Add a safety valve

Plan for real exceptions such as emergencies or travel. Keep essential apps outside the lock so you are never stuck, and review the setup weekly.

Step 8: Review after a week

Look at what worked. Did you finish the tasks sooner? Did you find a way around the block? Adjust the list, the apps or the timing and run it again.

Practical examples

Student

Tasks: one 45-minute study block and reviewing notes. Locked: short video apps, social media, games. Unlock: as soon as both are done, usually before dinner.

Remote worker

Tasks: finish the top priority, clear the inbox once. Locked: social media and news apps until the top priority is complete.

Morning routine

Tasks: drink water, stretch, write the day's plan. Locked: everything entertaining until the routine is done, so the day starts on your terms.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Too many tasks. A long list turns the system into a punishment.
  • Vague tasks. If you cannot tell when you are done, you cannot unlock anything.
  • Locking essential apps. Keep calls, messages, maps and work tools available.
  • An easy bypass. If you can disable the rule instantly, it will not hold under stress.
  • Cheating on the tasks. Ticking things off without doing them defeats the point.
