Gamified productivity apps borrow ideas from games, such as points, levels, streaks, rewards and friendly competition, to make habits and tasks feel less like chores. They do not work for everyone, but for people who get bored of plain checklists they can be the difference between quitting in week two and sticking with it. Here are six apps that use game mechanics in different ways.

What to look for

  • What you are gamifying: tasks, long-term habits, focus time, or self-care. Each app leans toward one.
  • Reward design: look for rewards tied to real progress, not just opening the app.
  • Consequences: some apps reward only, others also penalize misses. Choose what motivates you rather than stresses you.
  • Social features: friends, groups and leaderboards add accountability.
  • Reminders and tracking: a game layer still needs solid habit tracking underneath.
  • Free access and platforms: pricing and limits change, so check each store page.

The list

1. Habitica

Habitica is the classic role-playing take on productivity. Your habits, dailies and to-dos become part of a game where you build an avatar, earn rewards and face in-game consequences when you miss tasks. You can join parties with friends for shared challenges.

Best for: people who love fantasy and RPG mechanics. Our pick for a full role-playing experience.

2. Upshift

Upshift gamifies real life without turning it into a fantasy world. Your habits become quests, and finishing them earns XP in four categories: Money, Strength, Health and Knowledge. XP raises your global level, unlocks badges and achievements, and shows up on your profile. Daily sidequests, generated by the app, offer extra XP and variety. Quest types include one day, weekly, monthly, quit and roulette quests, and streak celebrations appear when you stay consistent.

The twist is that the game has real stakes for your phone: the Quest Blocker keeps distracting apps blocked until your daily quests are done. You can also add friends, join groups with shared challenges and climb leaderboards, pick from 9 themes, and use home screen widgets. Goals with a journal and a time tracker round it out. Upshift is on the App Store and Google Play and offers a 7-day free trial. Its blocking is built on Apple Screen Time; on Android, check the store page for what is supported.

Best for: people who want XP and levels for real habits, plus a reward that actually matters: access to their apps.

3. Forest

Forest gamifies focus. You plant a virtual tree and it grows as long as you stay off your phone. Leave early and the tree dies. Over time you build a forest of your focused sessions.

Best for: students and anyone who needs help with short focus sessions.

4. Finch

Finch pairs a virtual pet with self-care. You complete small daily goals and check in on your mood, and your pet grows and explores. The tone is gentle and avoids heavy pressure, which suits people who find streak-based apps stressful.

Best for: people who want a kind, low-pressure game around wellbeing and routines.

5. Todoist

Todoist is a well-known task manager with a clean interface and a points and levels system called Karma, which rewards completing tasks and keeping up productive streaks. The game layer is light, so it suits people who mainly want a strong to-do list with a small motivational push.

Best for: people who want a serious task manager with a touch of gamification.

6. Streaks

Streaks is not a full game, but its entire design is built on the streak mechanic: do your habits daily and keep the chain going. It is built for Apple devices and is deliberately minimal.

Best for: people who find a simple unbroken chain motivating, without avatars or story.

How to choose

Pick the game layer that fits your personality, not the one that sounds most fun in a description. If you enjoy stories and avatars, Habitica will keep you playing. If you want your progress to feel like leveling up your own life, Upshift's XP categories and profile are closer to that. If what you need is better focus, Forest is simple and fast. If you want something kind and calm, try Finch.

Also think about whether the reward is meaningful. Points alone fade quickly. Rewards that change your day, such as unlocking apps you want to use after finishing your tasks, tend to last longer. And social features matter: friends and leaderboards add accountability that a solo game cannot.

FAQ

Does gamification really improve productivity?

It helps many people stay consistent because progress feels visible and rewarding. It works best when the rewards are tied to real outcomes. It is not a fit for everyone, so test one app for a couple of weeks.

What is the difference between a gamified app and a regular habit tracker?

A regular tracker records what you did. A gamified app adds points, levels, rewards or competition to make you want to do it.

Are there free gamified productivity apps?

Some, including Habitica and Finch, are known to offer free access, but terms change and some features may be paid. Verify on the store page. Upshift offers a 7-day free trial.

Can I compete with friends?

Yes. Habitica has parties, and Upshift lets you add friends, share progress, join groups with shared challenges and compete on leaderboards.

Which app combines gamification with app blocking?

Upshift. Its Quest Blocker keeps distracting apps blocked until your daily quests are done.
