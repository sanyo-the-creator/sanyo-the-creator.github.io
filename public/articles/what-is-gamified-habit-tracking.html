Gamified habit tracking applies game mechanics, such as quests, XP, levels and streaks, to everyday habits. Instead of ticking boxes on a plain checklist, you complete quests, earn points and watch your progress grow. Upshift is built around this idea and adds app blocking on top.

Last updated November 2026.

The Core Idea

A normal habit tracker records what you did. A gamified habit tracker also gives feedback the way a game does: you complete a task, you get a reward, and you can see your progress build over time. The habits are the same. The structure around them is different.

The Building Blocks

Quests

A quest is a habit or task framed as something you complete. In Upshift, quests are your main daily habits that you want to build long-term. Naming a habit as a quest makes it concrete: it is either done or not done today.

Sidequests

Sidequests are optional extras. In Upshift they are daily challenges generated by the app, offering extra XP and keeping things interesting.

XP (experience points)

XP is the points you earn for completing quests. In Upshift, XP goes into one of four categories: Money, Strength, Health or Knowledge. This lets you see which areas of life you are putting effort into.

Levels

XP raises your global level. A level is a simple way to see long-term progress without having to remember every individual day.

Streaks

A streak counts how many days in a row you stayed consistent. It gives you a reason not to skip a day. In Upshift, streak and day celebrations pop up when you stay consistent or finish your daily goals.

Badges and achievements

Milestones unlock badges and achievements, which show up on your profile with the rest of your stats.

Leaderboards and groups

Social features add accountability. In Upshift you can add friends, share progress, and join groups with shared challenges and leaderboards.

Why It Can Work

We are not citing studies or statistics here, only explaining the general reasoning behind the approach.

  • Clear, small actions. A quest is a specific thing you can finish today, which is easier to start than a vague intention.
  • Immediate feedback. Earning XP right after finishing a task makes progress visible right away, instead of weeks later.
  • Visible progress. Levels and streaks turn effort into something you can see.
  • Light competition and accountability. When friends can see your progress, skipping is a little harder.
  • Variety. Different quest types and sidequests keep a routine from feeling repetitive.

It is not magic. If a habit is wrong for you or too large, points will not fix that. Gamification works best as a support for habits you already want to build.

Where Gamification Can Go Wrong

  • Chasing points instead of the habit. The goal is the habit. XP is a tool.
  • Too many quests. A huge list becomes a chore. Start with a few.
  • Streak pressure. A broken streak should not make you quit. Start again the next day.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is gamified habit tracking?

It is habit tracking that uses game elements such as quests, XP, levels and streaks to make progress visible and rewarding.

What categories of XP does Upshift have?

Money, Strength, Health and Knowledge.

What is the difference between quests and sidequests?

Quests are your main daily habits for the long term. Sidequests are daily challenges generated by the app that give extra XP and variety.

What happens when I keep a streak?

Streak and day celebrations pop up, and milestones unlock badges and achievements.

How is Upshift different from a normal habit tracker?

It combines quests and XP with an app blocker. Distracting apps can stay blocked until your daily quests are done.
