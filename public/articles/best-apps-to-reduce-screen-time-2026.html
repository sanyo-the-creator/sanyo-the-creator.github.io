To cut screen time for good you need two things: awareness of where your time goes, and a way to act on it. Some apps show you the numbers, some add friction, and some block apps outright. This list covers seven tools that approach the problem differently so you can pick the one that matches your habits.

What to look for

  • Usage insights: you cannot fix what you cannot see. Look for per-app usage and daily totals.
  • Limits and blocks: daily time limits, schedules and instant blocks turn good intentions into rules.
  • Friction vs hard walls: a short pause can be enough for some people, while others need a block they cannot easily dismiss.
  • Replacement habits: reducing screen time works better when you have something to do instead. Habit and goal features help.
  • Reminders and progress: streaks and history keep you motivated.
  • Free access and platforms: check each store page for current pricing and device support.

The list

1. Apple Screen Time

Built into iPhone, Screen Time shows daily and weekly usage, lets you set App Limits and Downtime, and can restrict content. It is free to use and a good place to see your baseline. Because you control the settings yourself, limits are easy to dismiss unless you add a passcode someone else holds.

Best for: getting your first honest look at your usage with nothing to install. See our guide to limiting screen time on iPhone.

2. Digital Wellbeing (Android)

Digital Wellbeing is Google's built-in tool for many Android phones. It shows how you use your device and lets you set app timers and focus modes. Exact features vary by phone and Android version, so check your device settings.

Best for: Android users who want a built-in starting point.

3. Upshift

Upshift attacks screen time from the habit side. Its Quest Blocker keeps distracting apps blocked until you complete your daily quests, so you earn your screen time instead of spending it by default. You can also see your total usage per app, then set up Time Limits for apps and websites, a Work Schedule, adult content and gambling blocks, or Block Now with one tap. With strict mode on, a block cannot be bypassed until the app is unblocked (depending on the block type).

It is built on Apple's Screen Time technology, so it cannot view your screen time data. Alongside blocking you get quests with XP, goals and a journal, a time tracker with a day streak, and friends, groups and leaderboards. Upshift is on the App Store and Google Play and offers a 7-day free trial; blocking on Android is not identical to iPhone, so check the store page.

Best for: people who want to replace scrolling with progress. Our pick for combining blocking with habits.

4. One Sec

One Sec adds a short pause and breathing moment before a chosen app opens. That moment of friction is often enough to interrupt automatic opening. You can still continue if you choose to.

Best for: mindless, reflexive app opening.

5. ScreenZen

ScreenZen introduces delays and prompts when you open selected apps and supports usage limits. It is awareness-oriented and is known for having a free option. Verify features and pricing on the store page.

Best for: people who want gentle limits and want to start for free.

6. Opal

Opal is a focus-oriented app that blocks distracting apps during sessions and schedules, with insights into your usage and focus. It is built around the idea of protecting deep work time.

Best for: people who want to protect focus sessions. Check the store page for platform support and pricing.

7. Forest

Forest grows a virtual tree while you stay away from your phone and ends the tree if you leave the app. It is a motivational timer rather than a strict block, and works well for short, focused stretches.

Best for: students and anyone who likes simple, visual motivation.

How to choose

Start by measuring. Spend a few days with the built-in tool on your phone (Screen Time on iPhone, Digital Wellbeing on many Android phones) and note your top three apps. Then match a method to your behavior:

  • If you open apps on autopilot, add friction with One Sec or ScreenZen.
  • If you keep ignoring your own limits, use stricter blocks, such as Upshift with strict mode or Opal.
  • If you need a reason to stay off your phone, a reward system like Forest or Upshift's XP can help.

Do not try to cut everything at once. Pick one app that costs you the most time and set a block or limit on it first. You can learn how in our guides to blocking apps on iPhone and blocking TikTok. For the evidence on why blocks work, read how app blockers work.

FAQ

How can I see how much I use my phone?

Use the built-in tool: Screen Time on iPhone or Digital Wellbeing on many Android phones. Upshift also shows total usage per app.

What is a healthy amount of screen time?

There is no single number that fits everyone. A better question is whether your usage matches what you want to spend time on. Use your own goals as the benchmark.

Do screen time apps actually work?

They work best when the rule is hard to ignore and tied to a goal. Hard blocks and strict modes tend to beat reminders alone.

Are there free apps to reduce screen time?

Apple Screen Time and Digital Wellbeing are built in. Some third-party apps offer free tiers or trials, but verify on each store page. Upshift offers a 7-day free trial.

Is my data safe?

Apps built on Apple's Screen Time technology work within Apple's privacy design. Upshift is built this way and cannot view your screen time data.
