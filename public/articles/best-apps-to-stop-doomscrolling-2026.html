Doomscrolling is the habit of scrolling endlessly through feeds, usually bad news or short videos, long after you meant to stop. Willpower alone rarely fixes it because feeds are designed to keep you there. The most effective apps either add friction before you open a feed, cap your time, or block it until you have done something more important. Here are seven tools to consider.

What to look for

  • Friction: a pause or prompt before an app opens can break the autopilot reflex.
  • Hard limits: daily time limits and schedules that are difficult to dismiss.
  • Conditions: blocking an app until you complete something else turns a distraction into a reward.
  • Website coverage: a lot of scrolling happens in the browser, not in apps.
  • Replacement habits: the urge does not vanish. Give your hands something else to do.
  • Free access and platforms: check each store page for pricing and device support.

The list

1. One Sec

One Sec inserts a short pause and a breathing moment before a chosen app opens. That moment is often enough to ask yourself whether you actually want to scroll. You can still continue, so it is about awareness rather than force.

Best for: automatic, reflexive opening of social apps. Our pick for friction.

2. Upshift

Upshift stops doomscrolling by putting something in front of it. Its Quest Blocker keeps distracting apps blocked until you finish your daily quests, so the feed becomes a reward for doing what matters first. For harder limits, you can set Time Limits for apps and websites, use Block Now to block apps instantly when you need to focus, or run a Work Schedule during working hours. With strict mode on, a block cannot be bypassed until the app is unblocked (depending on the block type), which matters when you are tired and tempted.

The app also gives you something to do instead: quests with XP, quit quests to track habits you want to break, goals with a journal, a time tracker, and friends and leaderboards for accountability. It is built on Apple's Screen Time technology, so it cannot view your screen time data. Upshift is on the App Store and Google Play and offers a 7-day free trial. Android blocking is not identical to iPhone, so check the store page.

Best for: people who need a firm rule and a replacement routine, not just a reminder.

3. Opal

Opal is a focus app that blocks distracting apps during sessions and on schedules, with usage insights. It is built to protect focused time and is a good choice if you want blocking to be part of a structured focus routine.

Best for: people who want scheduled focus sessions. Check the store page for current platforms and pricing.

4. ScreenZen

ScreenZen adds a delay and a mindful prompt when you open apps you choose, and supports limits on them. It is known for offering a free option and is aimed at building awareness. Verify the current features on the store page.

Best for: a lightweight, free-to-start approach to feed awareness.

5. Apple Screen Time

The built-in iPhone tool lets you set App Limits on social and video apps and schedule Downtime. It is free and already on your phone. The weakness is that you can usually dismiss a limit yourself, so it works best for people who are honest with themselves or who set a passcode held by someone else. See our guide to limiting screen time on iPhone.

Best for: trying limits first without installing anything.

6. Freedom

Freedom blocks apps and websites on a schedule or for a session, and can apply blocks across multiple devices. That helps if you doomscroll on your phone and then switch to a laptop.

Best for: people who scroll on several devices.

7. Headspace

Headspace is a meditation and mindfulness app. It does not block anything, but short guided sessions can help you notice the urge to scroll and respond to it differently. Calm is a similar option. These work as a companion to a blocker rather than a replacement.

Best for: people whose scrolling is driven by stress or anxiety.

How to choose

Match the tool to how you scroll. If you open feeds without thinking, friction apps like One Sec or ScreenZen are a good start. If you open them on purpose and lose track of time, set a time limit or a schedule. If you keep overriding your own rules, you need stricter blocks such as Upshift with strict mode or Opal.

Next, plan what replaces scrolling. A rule is easier to keep when you know your next action: a short walk, a quest, a page of a book. Tying access to completed tasks, as Upshift does with quests, handles that automatically. FAQ

Why is doomscrolling so hard to stop?

Feeds are built to be endless, with no natural stopping point. Adding friction, limits or conditions gives you the stopping point the feed lacks.

Should I delete the apps instead?

Deleting works for some people. If you need the app for other reasons, blocking or limiting it is a better balance. Blocks can be scheduled so the app is available when you need it.

Can I still use the app after a block?

Yes, in most setups. With Upshift's Quest Blocker, apps unlock once your daily quests are done. With strict mode on, a block cannot be bypassed until the app is unblocked (depending on the block type).

Are there free options?

Apple Screen Time is free and built in. Some third-party apps offer free tiers, but terms change, so verify on the store page. Upshift offers a 7-day free trial.

Does blocking websites matter too?

Yes, because feeds also live in the browser. Upshift's Time Limits cover apps and websites. See our guide to blocking websites on iPhone.
