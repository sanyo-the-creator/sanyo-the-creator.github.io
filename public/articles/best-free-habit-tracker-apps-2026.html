You can build good habits without paying anything, but "free" means different things in different apps. Some are fully free, some have a free tier with paid extras, and some offer a trial. This list covers seven habit trackers and explains in general terms what to expect. App pricing and limits change often, so always check the current store page before you rely on it.

What to look for in a free habit tracker

  • What is actually free: a free tier may cap the number of habits or lock some features. Read the store page.
  • Trial vs free: a free trial is temporary. A free tier lasts. Know which you are getting.
  • Habits vs tasks: decide if you need repeating habits, one-time tasks, or both.
  • Reminders and progress tracking: these are the core features, so make sure they are included.
  • Platforms: confirm the app runs on iPhone, Android or both.
  • Extras: focus tools, goals, or app blocking can add value if you need them.

The list

1. Habitica

Habitica turns habits, dailies and to-dos into a role-playing game and is known for having a free tier. You build an avatar, earn rewards and can join parties. Some extras are offered as optional paid features, so verify what you get.

Best for: people who like game mechanics and want to start free.

2. Loop Habit Tracker

Loop is a popular, no-frills habit tracker for Android that is known for being free and open source, with clear charts of your habit strength over time. It is simple and focused. It is not available on iPhone, so it is an Android option only.

Best for: Android users who want a simple, free tracker with good statistics.

3. Upshift

Upshift is not a free-forever app, but it offers a 7-day free trial that lets you try everything before you decide. It turns habits into quests with XP in Money, Strength, Health and Knowledge, with one day, weekly, monthly, quit and roulette quests, smart reminders, streak celebrations and badges. The Quest Blocker keeps distracting apps blocked until your daily quests are done, and you can add Block Now, Time Limits and a Work Schedule. Goals with a journal, a time tracker, friends, groups and leaderboards are included too. It is on the App Store and Google Play.

Best for: people who want to test a habit tracker that also controls phone distractions, and who are happy to try it free for a week first. Our pick if blocking is part of your plan.

4. Finch

Finch is a self-care app built around a virtual pet that grows as you complete small daily tasks. It is known to offer free access to its core experience, with optional paid features. The tone is gentle and suits people who feel overwhelmed by strict trackers.

Best for: low-pressure, self-care-style habits. Verify what is included in the free version.

5. Habitify

Habitify is a clean, dedicated habit tracker with reminders and progress statistics, available on several platforms. It is known to offer a free tier, though limits apply, so check the store page for what is included.

Best for: people who want a polished tracker and are fine with a limited free version.

6. TickTick

TickTick is a task manager with a built-in habit tracker and focus timer. It is known to have a free tier, with some features reserved for paid plans. It is a good fit if you want tasks, a calendar and basic habits in one app.

Best for: people who want one free app for tasks and basic habits.

7. Todoist

Todoist is a well-known task manager with a free tier. It is not a dedicated habit tracker, but recurring tasks let you create daily or weekly habits, and its simple design makes it easy to stick to. Check the store page for current free-plan limits.

Best for: people who want repeating tasks as habits inside a strong to-do app.

How to choose

Start by deciding what you cannot do without. If it is simply a checklist with reminders, nearly any free tier will do. If you want motivation, try Habitica or Finch. If you want habits tied to phone control, try Upshift's 7-day free trial and see if the Quest Blocker changes how you use your phone.

Before you commit, open the store page of each app and read what the free version includes. Look for limits on the number of habits, history length, widgets and reminders. These details change over time, and an app that was fully free last year may not be today.

Whatever you choose, give it two weeks. A free app that you use beats a paid app that you do not. To make habits easier to keep, you may also want to limit your screen time on iPhone and read why you feel unmotivated. You can also learn how to block apps on iPhone for free using built-in tools.

FAQ

Is there a completely free habit tracker?

Some apps, such as Loop Habit Tracker on Android, are known to be free. Others use a free tier with optional paid features. Verify on the store page.

What is the difference between a free tier and a free trial?

A free tier stays free with limits. A free trial gives full access for a short period. Upshift offers a 7-day free trial.

Can I use my phone's built-in apps as a habit tracker?

You can use reminders and recurring tasks, but they lack streaks, history and motivation features that dedicated trackers provide.

How many habits should I start with?

Start with one to three. Consistency matters more than volume.

Do free habit trackers work on both iPhone and Android?

Many do, including Habitica, Finch, Habitify, TickTick, Todoist and Upshift. Loop is Android only. Always confirm on the store page.
